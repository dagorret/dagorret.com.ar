Inflation has memory. Contracts, wage reviews, and pricing decisions incorporate recent outcomes and expectations. Their interaction can sustain inertia after the initial impulse changes. The chart makes that trajectory visible; explaining its causes requires additional information about fiscal policy, money, exchange rates, and expectations.

## Two speeds in the same economy

Monthly inflation compares the CPI with the previous month. Year-on-year inflation compares it with the same month a year earlier: it compounds twelve monthly changes rather than adding them. Monthly disinflation can therefore coexist with a high year-on-year rate for some time.

Monetary regimes matter because they shape expectations about money creation, exchange rates, and policy responses. A regime change may alter expectations while existing contracts and delayed adjustments continue to operate. A time series helps identify accelerations and slowdowns; it does not, by itself, identify the causal effect of each policy.

## Reading the scales

Linear scale preserves differences in percentage points. Logarithmic scale displays proportional distances: moving from 2% to 4% takes the same space as moving from 20% to 40%. It applies to positive inflation rates, not to the price-index level. Switch between them to distinguish the size of spikes from developments at lower inflation rates.

The chart uses the national headline CPI, with December 2016 = 100. Available monthly changes run from January 2017 through September 2026. Year-on-year observations begin in December 2017; unpublished observations are not estimated. Rates retain the official file’s one-decimal rounding. This is a dated local snapshot, not an automatically updated feed.

## Technical conclusion

A lower inflation rate means prices are rising more slowly, not returning to an earlier level. Assessing stabilization requires examining persistent monthly disinflation together with its transmission to year-on-year inflation. A high-inflation month leaving the twelve-month comparison window can also reduce the year-on-year rate without an equivalent change in current monthly inflation.
