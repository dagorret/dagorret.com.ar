A neural network transforms numbers through weighted sums and nonlinear functions. This simulation exposes a network with two inputs, three hidden neurons, and one output. Adjust the inputs to inspect the calculation, choose a target, and run a training step to change the weights.

## The forward pass

Each neuron combines its inputs with weights and a bias. For one layer, the operation is:

$$
y = \sigma(W \cdot x + b).
$$

Here we distinguish the hidden activation $\phi$ from the sigmoid output:

$$
h = \phi(W_1x+b_1), \qquad \hat y = \sigma(W_2h+b_2).
$$

The **forward pass** evaluates these operations in order. Changing an input changes the sums, activations, and prediction while keeping the weights fixed. Training is a separate operation.

## Sigmoid and ReLU

Sigmoid maps any real number to a value between zero and one. ReLU preserves positive values and sets negative values to zero:

$$
\sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}, \qquad \operatorname{ReLU}(z)=\max(0,z).
$$

The selector changes the three hidden neurons. The output always uses sigmoid. Node intensity represents activation, while connections show the magnitude and sign of the contribution $w\,a$, not a probability or causal explanation. Changing an input’s sign can change the contribution’s sign even if its weight remains unchanged.

## One gradient-descent step

For a target $t$, the loss on this example is:

$$
\mathcal L=\frac12(\hat y-t)^2, \qquad \theta_{k+1}=\theta_k-\eta\,\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal L.
$$

The button computes derivatives using **backpropagation** and updates both layers’ weights and biases simultaneously. If the chosen learning rate fails to reduce the loss, it tries a smaller step. The interface reports the loss before and after the update and the effective learning rate. ReLU uses a zero derivative at the origin; an inactive neuron can receive a zero gradient.

Each click trains on **the visible example**, not on a dataset. A lower loss here does not demonstrate generalization. Reset restores the initial inputs, weights, and target so the experiment can be repeated.
